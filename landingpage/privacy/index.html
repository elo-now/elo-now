Privacy policy

elo.now is a platform for team communication built around locally held identities and separate Spaces. Message and attachment content is encrypted, but running the service still requires processing personal data and operational metadata. This policy explains that distinction.

Which service are you using?

Available features and providers depend on your app version and the service configured for each Space. Installing an update does not move existing Spaces to a different server. The newer deployment supports a separate authorization witness and owner-configured attachment storage. Supported versions also let you import an independent operator’s signed hosting configuration and select it for a new Space. That configuration identifies public service endpoints, verification keys and available policies; it does not contain provider storage credentials. Import it only from an administrator you trust. Older Spaces may still use publisher-configured MEGA storage and require an owner device to be online for admission. Independent operators choose their own infrastructure and must explain their processing separately.

Earlier version-1 hosted General used an enrollment-service reader credential. Existing Spaces on that legacy implementation can therefore expose General content to their operator; updating the app alone does not revoke those keys. Current owner-managed Spaces keep General decryption keys on participant devices.

Your profile and device

You choose a name and create a local cryptographic identity. Profile information, contacts, conversation history, preferences and cryptographic material are held on your device. The local password protects your saved profile. Optional biometric unlocking uses the operating system; elo does not receive your face or fingerprint template.

Your public identity, device credential and chosen profile information are shared where needed to identify you to conversation participants and Space services. Do not send your password, recovery words or recovery QR to support.

Messages and Spaces

Messages are encrypted on the sending device before transmission to storage services called Replicas. Authorized participants decrypt them on their devices. A Space groups membership, General and other conversations; joining a Space does not automatically grant access to every private chat or to earlier General history. Service signing and transport keys are not participant keys for decrypting message content.

Encryption does not prevent an authorized participant from saving, exporting or sharing received content, including sending it to an external AI service. A compromised participant device can expose readable content. The current app has no integration that submits conversations to an AI service on the publisher’s behalf. These limits are different from whether an infrastructure operator holds content-decryption keys.

Data processed by our services

Publisher-operated services process the following data, including where message content is encrypted:

  • Network information: IP addresses are visible during connections and may appear in error and security logs. Address or network hashes and request counters help limit abuse. A hash is not a guarantee of anonymity.
  • Delivery metadata: object and route identifiers, sizes, storage times, queue state and delivery attempts support synchronization, retries and storage limits. A storage receipt does not prove that a person received or read a message.
  • Notification identifiers: Firebase installation or registration identifiers, account bindings, delivery routes, preferences and unread-attention state support push delivery, muting, badges and removal of registrations.
  • Membership and access information: public profile identifiers, chosen names, Space membership, roles, invitations, join requests and access decisions let services check permissions.
  • Device information: public device identifiers and keys, signed credentials and their association with a profile and its permissions support device approval, revocation and recovery. These are not your private signing keys.
  • Storage configuration: where the owner enables attachments, the storage broker processes the chosen provider, restricted storage credentials, object identifiers, sizes, deadlines and transfer state.

An optional introduction note in a signed join request is visible to the approval service and owner. It is removed from the queue’s stored applicant record after a decision; replay responses may retain it for up to two minutes. A hosted Space’s primary owner supplies a contact email for membership and data requests. Current members can see it; it is replaced when a new primary owner accepts the role with a replacement address. Creating a profile or joining as a member does not require an email address.

Authorization witness and invitations

On deployments that support it, a separately configured witness validates and records covered authorization changes against owner-signed policies. An eligible open invitation can be used while the owner is offline; approval-required invitations still require the owner’s decision. The witness processes public authority proofs, invitation policies, device and membership identifiers, counters and signed receipts. It is not a message-content store and does not need message-decryption keys.

A shortened Space invitation can reference an encrypted descriptor on the API. The invitation’s decryption material remains in the shared link, not in that stored descriptor. Whoever receives a usable open invitation may use it within its policy and validity period. Share links only with intended recipients; owner approval provides an additional access decision. The self-contained My code contact card is read from the code itself and is not stored as a server-hosted contact card by this feature.

The witness and API have separate service keys and access controls. This separation does not guarantee protection against compromise of participants, both services or their shared infrastructure administration. In the publisher’s newer deployment, the witness and storage broker run as separate services on the same host; a compromise of that host could affect both roles.

Attachments and storage providers

Files are encrypted on your device before upload. Authorized participants receive the file-decryption key inside an encrypted message and can decrypt and save the file. Attachment bytes are not included in conversation backups.

Where owner-configured storage is available, the Space owner enables attachments during creation or in Space settings and connects a dedicated MEGA folder using its folder link and write key, or an S3-compatible bucket using restricted credentials. MEGA is a third-party cloud service; an owner’s account is not a server physically controlled by that owner. A hosting operator may instead offer managed attachment storage, with provider credentials configured on its storage broker. The app requests this configuration during Space creation without asking the owner to enter provider credentials.

Provider credentials are sent to and retained by the separate storage broker, which uses them to upload, retrieve and delete encrypted objects. The API does not receive those storage credentials in this flow. The broker can use them and is therefore a trusted holder of storage access, even though it does not receive the file-decryption keys. The provider sees the account or bucket, encrypted objects and transfer metadata. Disabling storage stops new uploads; existing files remain downloadable until their own deadlines. Changing provider applies to new uploads, while existing objects retain their original provider and deadline. Credentials for an older configuration remain while retained objects need them, including for deletion; they become eligible for removal once no object or active configuration needs them. Revoking those credentials at the provider can prevent downloads and cleanup.

Older publisher-operated Spaces may use MEGA configured by the publisher. They do not automatically acquire owner-configured storage after an app update. Applicable file-size, quota and expiry limits are shown by the app or service.

Optional system notifications

When you enable system notifications, elo uses Google Firebase Cloud Messaging and, on Apple devices, Apple Push Notification service. These providers process installation or registration identifiers and delivery information. elo’s notification service stores the device registration and opaque routing, preference and unread-attention information needed to deliver and quiet alerts. A signed public account identifier links registrations from the same account so they can be deleted together.

Notification routing targets are encrypted. Message text and conversation names are not included in the current generic system notification payload. You can disable system notifications in Settings or mute a conversation. OS settings may also control delivery.

See Firebase’s privacy information and Apple’s privacy policy. Their processing and international transfers must be considered alongside those of your Space operator.

Live updates and activity indicators

An authenticated live connection signals available updates. Typing, foreground online status and attachment-upload indicators are signed and encrypted for conversation participants. They expire and are not added to message history or sent as system notifications. The server still observes connection addresses, timing, routing and traffic sizes.

App updates

Versions with update checking request a public release policy when the app starts, without sending a profile identifier or device credential. The server receives ordinary network information such as the IP address. The policy is cached locally and may require an update through an official download page.

Permissions

Camera access is used when you choose to scan a QR, take a photo or video, set a profile photo, or enable video in a chat session. Microphone access is used when you choose to start or join an audio session in a chat. Desktop screen recording is used only when you choose to share your screen. File and photo access supports the files or images you choose to open, share or save. System notifications and biometric unlocking are optional. Refusing an optional permission does not stop you using the rest of the app.

Live chat sessions

You choose to start or join an audio session from an open chat. Camera and desktop screen sharing are enabled separately at your choice. On supported mobile devices, audio may continue when you switch apps or lock the screen until you leave the session or sign out; Android displays an ongoing session notification. Operating-system restrictions, permissions and connectivity can interrupt a session.

Live chat sessions use a separate service for signaling and media delivery. Other participants can see your chosen name and media you enable during the session. Session services and network providers process connection metadata, including network addresses, timing and participation. On compatible networks, media may travel directly between participants; relays can carry media when a direct connection is unavailable. The app does not record sessions for the publisher.

Backups and recovery

Recovery codes restore your identity; they are not a copy of chat history. Exported encrypted backups include supported profile and conversation data, but not attachment bytes. The backup budget is 64 MiB before encryption and compression; older message groups may be omitted to fit. The app reports omissions. You control where an exported backup or recovery QR is saved and shared.

Website, support and diagnostics

This static website uses locally hosted fonts and images. Its supplied code has no advertising, analytics scripts or tracking cookies. The web host may process normal request and security logs. If you contact support, the provider receives the contact information and material you choose to send. Avoid including unnecessary conversation content or secrets.

The app does not include advertising or behavioral analytics. In beta builds with Firebase Crashlytics, enabling Debug in Settings also enables sending diagnostic reports to Google Firebase. Reports can include crash stack traces, error codes, operation stages and timing, app version, operating system and device information, and a random diagnostic installation identifier. Custom reports exclude message content, passwords, cryptographic keys, invitation links and request payloads. Automatic native crash reports are generated by the Firebase SDK. Reports are sent on a subsequent launch while Debug remains enabled. Disabling Debug stops sending and discards pending local reports; it does not delete reports already received by Firebase. This diagnostic processing is separate from message retention, notification permissions and the location of your Space server. Firebase applies its own retention and processing arrangements. Contact the publisher about reports already sent.

Automatic account deletion

Encrypted content carries signed public attribution identifying its publishing account and, for shared contact or history bundles, the accounts represented in the bundle. This lets the service remove the appropriate ciphertext without reading message text. The service keeps a minimal deletion marker and authority/revocation proofs to prevent removed access or content being restored. See the account deletion instructions for the in-app action and retained-copy limitations.

Why data is processed

For publisher-operated services, processing necessary to provide the communication, membership and support services you request is based on performance of the applicable service contract (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). Protecting accounts and infrastructure, limiting abuse and investigating security incidents serves our legitimate interests and those of users (Article 6(1)(f)). Where a law requires retention or disclosure, the basis is the relevant legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)). Where optional processing requires consent, consent is requested and may be withdrawn (Article 6(1)(a)); an operating-system permission is not by itself a description of every legal basis.

An organization using elo for work must identify its own purposes and lawful bases, inform its users and arrange any required processing agreements. Installing open-source software does not, by itself, establish compliance or make the publisher that organization’s processor for every purpose.

Personal blocking

Blocking is a private preference for your profile on this device, across its Spaces. Blocked users’ messages and reactions are hidden, and their personal requests are dismissed. The notification service verifies the sending device’s signed identity proof and checks a recipient-owned block list; it sees sender identity during verification but not message plaintext. It stores a route-specific sender tag for policy and queued delivery. The block list is included in encrypted backups and can be managed in More → Blocked users. Other devices require their own updated preferences. Blocking does not revoke another member’s access to messages in a shared channel.

Three different expiry settings

  • Server message lifetime: public elo.now hosting offers 6, 12 or 24 hours, with 24 hours selected by default. An imported private hosting profile can offer additional choices, including 48 hours or No expiry. The owner selects from that host’s available policies when creating the Space. A finite lifetime bounds availability of encrypted message bodies in server delivery storage; copies can be removed earlier, including after an accepting device acknowledges direct-message delivery. With No expiry, bodies and their encrypted locators have no automatic deadline and remain after that acknowledgement. Neither policy guarantees availability or unlimited capacity: quotas, explicit deletion and the operator’s storage and backup policies still apply. A server policy does not erase received history on every device. Copies that reach a finite deadline become unavailable for normal download and enter cleanup.
  • Keep — No expiry, 1 hour or 24 hours; default No expiry: this separate, optional message setting controls expiry of message content in participating elo apps. In the composer, ∞ means No expiry. After a timed deadline, the app removes the content and shows Message expired when it processes expiry. Older versions may call this setting Delete after or retain other previously supported deadlines. Keep: No expiry does not change the Space’s server policy or guarantee permanent availability; server No expiry does not disable a message’s Keep deadline.
  • Attachment expiry — 1, 12 or 24 hours; default 1 hour: the Space owner selects the download lifetime for new uploads. The deadline starts at upload reservation. Changing this setting does not rewrite earlier files’ deadlines. At expiry, new downloads are blocked and encrypted objects enter deletion at the storage provider. Provider or service failures can delay physical cleanup.

If you remain offline beyond the server delivery period, missing history may require another authorized participant with a retained copy to reconnect and synchronize. Retrieval is not guaranteed. Received history and cached attachments can remain after server expiry. Message expiry cannot erase screenshots, exports, files saved independently or copies outside elo, and cannot make an offline device process changes immediately.

Retention of other data

The settings above do not define the lifetime of all metadata, logs or backups. The current services use these separate periods and criteria:

  • Push delivery: ordinary queued targets and duplicate-prevention records expire within 24 hours. Session-start hints expire within 60 seconds. Device routes expire after 30 days without renewal; preference and unread-attention records are associated with the route and may remain until read, updated, removed or the route is cleaned up. Deletion and housekeeping remove eligible records. Firebase and Apple apply their own retention independently.
  • Membership, devices and permissions: current records remain while needed to operate a Space and determine access. Account or Space deletion removes supported membership and contact data. Minimal signed authority, revocation, receipt and deletion records may remain to reject replay or restoration of removed access; these safeguards do not have a message-style automatic expiry.
  • Invitations: encrypted short-link descriptors are eligible for cleanup at their stored expiry. Expiry or revocation prevents new admission, but does not erase authority history or an existing participant’s membership. Self-contained contact codes do not create a hosted contact-card record.
  • Abuse controls: daily request budgets reset or are pruned as the day changes. A network hash associated with Space creation can remain with that Space’s allocation record so active-Space limits survive restarts and daily resets. These hashes are not covered by the message expiry setting.
  • Storage access: object records and the credentials needed to reach their provider remain through expiry and successful cleanup. Retired provider credentials are eligible for removal once neither an active configuration nor any retained object requires them. Failed provider deletion keeps the cleanup record and can prolong this period.
  • Operational backups: encrypted recovery snapshots are separate from live delivery storage. Server staging rotation and independently retained recovery copies have separate lifecycles. A message or attachment deadline does not erase every snapshot. Independently pulled backups are not automatically purged by the server’s expiry job; their removal requires operator action based on recovery needs, pending deletion requests and applicable retention duties.
  • Logs and support: host and service logs follow separate rotation settings; incident evidence and support correspondence may be retained while needed to resolve a request, investigate an incident, meet a legal duty or address a claim. Their expiry is not controlled by a Space. Contact the publisher for the schedule and records applicable to your deployment.

Disconnecting a Space removes that Space’s local conversations and contacts from that device. Removing a local profile removes its local data. Neither action erases independent copies on other devices, exported backups or every server. See account and data deletion for the distinct server-side deletion action and retained-copy limitations.

Your choices and rights

Depending on applicable law, you may request access, correction, deletion, restriction or portability of personal data, object to certain processing, or withdraw consent. Contact elonow@9bits.com for publisher-operated services, or the administrator of the relevant company Space. You may also complain to your competent data protection authority. Requests must be verified proportionately; we will not ask for your recovery secret.

Recipients and international processing

Authorized participants receive the content you share with them. Space operators and authorized service administrators process the operational data described above. Publisher-operated infrastructure uses OVHcloud hosting; optional notifications use Google Firebase and Apple. Attachments use MEGA or the S3-compatible provider configured for the relevant Space and version. S3 compatibility does not necessarily mean Amazon operates that storage. Hosting, storage, notification and support providers process data needed for their respective services; competent authorities may receive information where lawfully required.

The newer publisher deployment uses servers in the European Union; older Spaces can remain on earlier infrastructure outside the EU. Notification providers and owner-selected storage can process data in other countries. A server’s location alone is not a promise that all processing stays in that country. Contact elonow@9bits.com for the provider arrangements, locations and applicable transfer safeguards for the service you use; independent Space operators must provide their own information. See Firebase’s privacy information and Apple’s privacy policy.

This policy will be updated when the product or processing changes; material changes will be communicated as required. A preview build, free service or testing arrangement does not remove applicable data-protection rights.
